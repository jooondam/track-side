# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary: a motorsport engineer evaluating the developer's capability.** Arrives from a link on
an application, has roughly six minutes, and is on a laptop (1440x900 is the modal review
device). They are judging two things at once: whether the vehicle dynamics are understood, and
whether the person can ship. They will look for a sign the work is rigorous and a sign it is not,
and a single visible inconsistency counts for more than a polished surface.

**Secondary: sim racers and drivers reading a circuit.** Want to know where the time goes at Spa
and why the line runs where it does. Repeat visitors, more interested in corners and braking
points than in methodology.

Where the two conflict, the engineer wins.

## Product Purpose

Computes an ideal racing line on real circuit geometry and makes it readable. A minimum-curvature
path solver and a velocity-profile solver run over measured centrelines and registered elevation;
the browser renders the circuit in 3D with the solved line, a car driving it at the solved v(s),
and the telemetry instruments an engineer would work from.

Success is that a reader can answer "where is this lap being lost, and why" and can check the
reasoning rather than having to trust it.

## Positioning

**It re-solves live in the browser.** The grip slider drives a real velocity solver at roughly
13 ms per drag, on a ~7000-point circuit, with the measured figure shown in the interface. The
TypeScript solver is a port of the Python implementation and is cross-validated against it to
0.1 m/s on both circuits.

This is the claim a neighbouring tool could not truthfully copy: most racing-line visualisations
animate a precomputed result. Moving grip here changes physics, not a playback speed.

## Operating Context

- Opened from a link, usually once, often on a laptop rather than a phone.
- Deployed as a static site on GitHub Pages (`jooondam.github.io/track-side/`). No backend, no
  live timing, no accounts.
- The viewer's state lives in the query string, so every view is linkable and reproducible:
  `?circuit=spa&view=corner:Eau%20Rouge&mu=1.0&ghost=1&enter=1`.
- `scripts/shots.mjs` drives the app through those same URLs for headless review, so there is no
  test hook and no privileged build. Any frame the harness captures is a frame a person can open.
- Assets under `/public` are generated by `offline/build_viewer_assets.py` from committed
  artifacts.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Two circuits: Spa-Francorchamps and Monza.
- Grip μ is adjustable from 0.6 to 1.4 and re-solves synchronously in low-single-digit
  milliseconds. No debouncing, no worker.
- **The racing line is grip-invariant by design** (`docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md` §0.1). The slider drives
  the velocity profile, not the path. Any future work that implies the line itself moves with grip
  is wrong.
- Instruments: v(s) trace with camera-linked corner ticks, delta to a ghost plotted against arc
  length rather than time, a g-g diagram over the tyre's own μ·g circle, and a per-corner report.
- Vehicle is a GT3 model: 1300 kg, 410 kW, μ 1.2, 2.8 m² downforce area, rear drive, 62% front
  brake bias.
- **Absolute lap times are estimated, not measured.** The GT3 g-g-v is estimated
  (`DESIGN_NOTES.md` assumptions register #5), so the solved lap time must never be presented as a
  credible real-world lap. What the number is good for is comparison between solves.
- Physics the model does not have: tyre thermal or wear behaviour, per-section friction variation,
  road camber, lateral load transfer, suspension dynamics, yaw balance from a slip-angle model.
  Twenty-three such assumptions are registered with what each one bites.
- Licensing constraint, binding: no F1 wordmarks, logos, or circuit device marks anywhere in the
  interface. Descriptive circuit names are fine. Attribution for TUMFTM (LGPL-3.0), OpenStreetMap
  (ODbL 1.0) and OpenF1 must remain present.

**Explicitly undecided:** whether the ghost's reference grip stays fixed or becomes user-selectable;
how (and whether) the vehicle model is surfaced in the interface, given it is currently never named.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **track-side**.
- Voice: concrete and specific, and it states its own limits in its own copy. Existing examples
  worth preserving as the register: "Eau Rouge climbs 40 m here because it does in Belgium", and
  the corner report saying outright that its corner window is a symmetric definition rather than a
  measurement. Adjective stacking is off-register.

## Evidence on Hand

Real, and available to use:

- TUMFTM `racetrack-database` centrelines and widths (LGPL-3.0), originally from OpenStreetMap.
- Elevation registered from 2023 OpenF1 car-location data: 2.6 m registration RMSE at Spa, zero
  rejected samples, reproducing the documented ~102 m range and Eau Rouge's ~40 m climb.
- Comparison against TUMFTM `global_racetrajectory_optimization` (Heilmeier et al., 2020) as a
  reference raceline, and an offline min-time NLP reference over a μ grid.
- Solver cross-validation: TypeScript against Python to 0.1 m/s on both circuits.
- Measured in-browser solve time, currently ~10 to 13 ms.
- A 23-entry assumptions register in `docs/DESIGN_NOTES.md` §6.
- A live deployment and a headless screenshot harness.

**Absences that future work must not fabricate:** there is no measured telemetry from a real GT3,
no validation of lap time against a real GT3 lap, no users, no testimonials, no benchmark against
any competing tool, and no claim of endorsement by any series, circuit or manufacturer.

## Product Principles

1. **The model's uncertainty is part of the output.** The g-g ring is drawn flat and the data is
   allowed to sit outside it. Clamping the picture to look tidy would destroy the thing that makes
   this credible.
2. **Interactivity is proof, not decoration.** Every interactive element should demonstrate that
   something real is being computed, or it should not move.
3. **The instruments answer "where", not only "how much".** The delta is plotted against distance
   for this reason; a single summary number is the weakest form of every readout here.
4. **Every view is a link.** State belongs in the URL, so any frame can be shared, reproduced and
   photographed without a special mode.
5. **Nothing on screen is fabricated.** Every number shown is read from a loaded artifact or a
   live solve, and anything estimated says so.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

**WCAG 2.2 AA is binding for this work**, not aspirational. It constrains the new visual world
from the start: contrast ratios (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large and non-text), visible focus for every
interactive element, full keyboard paths, target sizes at the 24x24 minimum, and no state or
phase conveyed by colour alone.

One product-specific requirement beyond the standard: the racing line's phase colouring is the
core encoding of the whole tool, so it must stay separable under deuteranopia, protanopia and
tritanopia. A red and green pairing fails this and cannot be used for phase.
